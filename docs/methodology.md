# Evidence-led investment decisions

Freeze the cutoff, budget, horizon and objective. Event, publication, filing acceptance, quote and retrieval times are distinct. Later retrieval cannot make a later event available at an earlier decision. Search-index text and a dynamic PDF may represent different versions.

Connect business economics to cash generation. Reconcile GAAP, issuer adjustments, analyst normalization and quarter annualization. Link reinvestment, working capital, leases, taxes, SBC, shares and acquisitions to owner cash. A low headline P/E is an investigative lead, not a valuation conclusion.

Apply consistent standards across stocks and funds. Diversification can support a decision, but ETF aggregate P/E is not audited EPS or a complete DCF. Dates, exclusions and aggregation conventions can differ even for funds tracking one index. Count fees once and measure underlying overlap.

Separate earnings growth, multiple change and interaction. Show an unchanged-multiple alternative. Cash valuation and market-price targets answer different questions; do not average them to conceal disagreement. Scenario labels are not calibrated probabilities.

Volatility tolerance does not eliminate permanent loss, funding or valuation risk. It also does not authorize replacing a requested equity portfolio with cash. Preserve conditional high-upside alternatives and opportunity costs.

When filings cannot answer recent events, search and read reporting, then trace claims to issuers, regulators, managers or agencies. Holdings are dated snapshots. Direct stock reductions do not capture ETF or derivative exposure. Distinguish rating outlook, rating notch and equity analyst opinion.

Treasury returns separate carry, duration, floating resets and execution costs. Annualized SEC yield is not a 30-day return and generally includes fund expenses. Distributions are not an extra windfall alongside price adjustments. Trade, settlement and withdrawable-cash dates differ.

Academic study follows independent analysis and cross-review. Challenge selection bias, multiple testing, factor timing, concentrated return distributions, costs and calibration. Lessons become versioned prompts or tests, not retrospective proof of stock-selection skill.

The ranking score is a disclosed preference, not a universal utility function. Inspect downside, valuation sensitivity and evidence quality. Reassess changed fundamentals and unresolved facts rather than describing an untested order as optimal.
